Free · Open source · macOS

Your open Kalshi sports bets. One window.

Spex Glance is a small, free, open-source Mac app that keeps tabs on your open sports positions on Kalshi. Current and upcoming games, what you paid, what each side is worth right now, what it pays if it hits, and since 0.3.0, what you've settled and how your P&L has gone. Think of the Stocks app, but for your Kalshi sports positions.

What it's for

Keep tabs without keeping a tab open.

Kalshi's portfolio page updates live too. It also needs a browser tab, a login, and the rest of the site around it. This is the same feed in a plain window you can leave open, or a number in the menu bar, without the browser.

By default it keeps almost nothing. Your API key lives in the macOS Keychain and your settings live with the app. Positions stream in and are shown, and the realized P&L chart is rebuilt from Kalshi's own settlement records on every launch. If you turn on Live Line, the app records your sports total over time so it can chart it, encrypted on that Mac or in your private iCloud, your choice. It's off until you say otherwise.

Up close. Start time, countdown, what it's worth now, what it pays, and the running P&L.

Features

What it does, and not much else.

Live from your own Kalshi account

Your own read-only API key, straight to Kalshi. One connection carries prices, fills, orders and results, so a change on Kalshi shows up in the window within a second or two. A five-minute refresh runs behind it as a safety net, and the live dot goes amber if the feed falls behind.

Kalshi health, in the menu bar

When Kalshi is having a bad day, the icon turns ember for degraded, red for down, gray for maintenance, with the reason and how long it's been going in the dropdown. Read from Kalshi's own status endpoint, so you stop wondering whether it's the app, your network, or them.

Work Mode

⇧⌘M hides the icon, the dollar sign, the money tiles and the buttons. What's left on screen is a list of games. Sharing your display stops being a disclosure.

Filter by sport, sort by match time

Baseball, hockey, tennis, and whatever else Kalshi tags as sports. One click to see only tonight's slate. Sort by Live first, earliest match, or latest, so what's on now sits at the top and tomorrow's early tennis waits its turn.

Combos, leg by leg

A multi-market combo shows as one card with every leg listed by sport and game, each leg's chance, and a check or cross as they settle. No mental math about which parlay is still alive.

Four sections, four shortcuts

Positions, Orders, Settled and P&L, on ⌘1 to ⌘4. Orders shows your resting orders and nothing more. Settled lists every sports market Kalshi has settled for you, newest first, grouped by day with the day's net, Won or Lost, and the fee.

Realized P&L, rebuilt from Kalshi

The P&L section draws your realized sports P&L as a step chart from Kalshi's settlement records. It's recomputed on every launch, so it's right from day one and nothing has to be stored for it. 1D, 7D, 30D or All, with a fees toggle.

Live Line, if you want it

Kalshi keeps no history of what your portfolio was worth, so the app can record one for you. Off by default. Turn it on in Settings and choose where the history lives: encrypted on this Mac only, or synced between your Macs through your private iCloud. Clear it or export it as CSV any time.

Read-only. Always.

It runs on a read-only Kalshi key and has no order code. It can't place, change or cancel a bet, and it won't accept a key that could.

What it isn't

It can't place a bet. That's the point.

Sports positions only. Anything else you hold is counted in the totals, not listed. It relays Kalshi's numbers and has no opinion about them.

Setup

Three steps. About five minutes.

Kalshi has no "sign in with Kalshi" for third-party apps, so connecting is a one-time manual step on kalshi.com. The app walks you through it.

  1. Download and open Spex GlanceGrab the latest release from GitHub, or brew install --cask spexbet/tap/spex-glance. It's notarized, so macOS opens it like any other app.
  2. Create a read-only API key on KalshiThe connect wizard points you at the right page on kalshi.com. Create a key with read-only permissions and drop the key file into the app.
  3. GlanceYour open sports positions show up grouped by game. Turn on the menu bar item if you want it, and get back to whatever you were doing.

FAQ

Questions people ask before installing a thing that touches their money.

Is this affiliated with Kalshi?

No. Spex Glance is an independent open-source project. It's not affiliated with, endorsed by, or supported by Kalshi. "Kalshi" is their trademark.

Where does my key go?

Into your macOS Keychain, on your Mac. The app talks directly to Kalshi's API. There's no Spex server in between.

What does it store on my Mac?

Your Kalshi API key, in the macOS Keychain, and your settings. That's it unless you turn on Live Line. With Live Line on, the app saves a history of your sports total so it can chart it: encrypted in the app's own folder if you pick "This Mac only" (Disconnect erases it), or in your private iCloud if you pick iCloud, which Apple encrypts and syncs between your Macs. Your key is never part of that history. "Clear history" deletes it, and switching off iCloud offers to delete the cloud copy too.

Are the numbers real time?

As current as Kalshi's ticker sends them. They can lag or be wrong. Confirm on Kalshi before acting on anything.

Does it collect anything?

Nothing. No analytics, no crash reports, no install counting. The app makes two kinds of network calls: to Kalshi's API for your positions, and to GitHub once a day to check for updates. A third appears only if you choose iCloud for Live Line history, and that goes to your own iCloud account, not to us. Details in the privacy note.

Does Kalshi have a Mac app or desktop app?

Not that we know of. Kalshi has a website and iPhone and Android apps. Spex Glance is an independent, read-only Mac app that shows your Kalshi sports positions using Kalshi's public API. It isn't made by Kalshi.

Does it work with Polymarket, FanDuel, DraftKings, or other sportsbooks?

No. Kalshi only. Spex Glance reads positions through Kalshi's API, and the other platforms have different APIs, different account models, and in the sportsbooks' case no public read API at all. Nothing planned there.

Is there a Windows version?

No, and none is being worked on. Spex Glance is a native Mac app, built with Apple's own frameworks for the menu bar, the widget and the Keychain, none of which carry over. If the Mac app finds enough people, a Windows version is possible down the road. Not promised, not planned.

Is there an iPhone version? A self-hosted one?

iOS is on the list, not shipped. Spex Board, a self-hosted web dashboard for homelabs, is in the works. Spex Glance works on its own and won't need it.

Can I get the intimate details of the inner workings of this?

Yes. All of it is on GitHub: the Swift source, the build scripts, the release process, and the README that explains how the app talks to Kalshi. MIT licensed, so you can read it, build it yourself, or fork it.

Why is it called Spex?

A haruspex was a Roman priest who predicted the future by reading the entrails of a sacrificed animal. That's roughly what checking your Kalshi portfolio at 11pm feels like. Spex is the tail end of that word, and also specs, as in the glasses in the logo. Less mess, same anxiety.
